The Gains from AI

A theoretical scenario study of AI-driven productivity growth and systemic instability

Scenario study with a reproducible quantitative core. Not a forecast: every probability is conditional on stated assumptions.
ImportantNature of this study

This is a theoretical scenario study. It is not a forecast. Every probability on this site is a conditional probability under the assumptions listed in the Assumptions Register, computed from a parameter file whose values are, at this version, mostly theoretical placeholders. Changing an assumption changes the numbers.

Does AI lift every income, or concentrate it? That is the question, and what this site offers is an answer you can audit rather than one you have to trust: five sectors, one transition model, 55 named assumptions of which 49 are still marked placeholder, 10,000 draws per horizon, and a parameter hash printed under every table, so any number here traces back to the exact choices that produced it.

The question

Under what conditions does AI-driven productivity growth diffuse welfare, and under what conditions does it produce systemic instability?

The study answers it by building an explicit structure in five sectors (labour, AI profitability, energy, public finance, political stability), seeding it with published statistics, sampling the theoretical choices, and reporting which regions of the resulting state space the samples land in.

What the numbers on this site are

A probability like 0.424 the share of 10,000 parameter draws whose simulated state at that horizon satisfies that scenario’s region
A 95% interval the sampling noise of that share (Wilson), not uncertainty about the world
“2031” and “2036” labels for t = 5 and t = 10 years after the 2026 reference year
Data World Bank WDI observations retrieved on 2026-10-28; latest observation years 2024-2025
Everything else assumptions, listed in the Assumptions Register

Reproducing and citing

The analysis source is not published. The transition model, the ingestion scripts, the parameter file and the retrieved data live in a private working repository, by the author’s decision. So the commands below document how the numbers were made; they are not a recipe a visitor can run.

python 02_analysis/scripts/ingest/build_processed.py   # retrieve and align the inputs
python 02_analysis/scripts/model/build_lookup.py       # 10,000 draws per horizon -> the JSON
quarto render 05_website                              # this site

What a reader can still check from here, without the source: the data file the page renders, data/scenario_lookup.json, is served exactly as it was computed, so the table in your browser is the table that came out of the model and not something re-derived on the fly; and every table prints the model_params_hash and model_code_hash of the inputs behind it. What you cannot check is the arithmetic in between. That part is the author’s claim, and it should be cited as one.

Quote a probability with its model_params_hash, model_code_hash and seed; without them you are quoting a number from a model you cannot name.
